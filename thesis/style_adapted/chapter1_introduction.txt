\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Context and Motivation}
\label{sec:context_motivation}

Football is a highly dynamic and uncertain environment, which makes decision making difficult even for experienced coaches and technical staff. Match outcomes are influenced by many interacting factors, including team quality, recent form, tactical style, match venue, rest periods, and short-term variation. Although modern football generates large amounts of statistical information, transforming this information into useful coaching decisions remains a difficult task. For this reason, football has become an important domain for machine learning and sports analytics, where historical data can be used to identify patterns that are not immediately visible through subjective analysis alone~\cite{rodrigues2022prediction,tacspinar2021improvement}.

Most existing football analytics systems focus primarily on prediction. Their main objective is to estimate the probability of a result, such as a win, draw, or loss, based on historical and contextual features. While this is useful, prediction alone does not fully address the practical needs of coaching staff. In a match-preparation setting, coaches do not only want to know what is likely to happen. They also want to understand why a prediction was produced and what tactical adjustments could improve the chance of a favourable outcome. The distinction between \emph{predictive} and \emph{prescriptive} analytics becomes important here. Predictive analytics informs decisions, whereas prescriptive analytics is intended to support or drive them more directly~\cite{houtmeyers2021managing,bertsimas2020predictive}. This thesis is built around that transition: its central goal is to design and implement a system that not only estimates match win probability, but also explains the main drivers behind it and recommends realistic tactical blueprints associated with a higher chance of success.

The practical outcome of this research is \textbf{UmbraRo}, a mobile application designed as an AI-powered tactical assistant for football coaches and technical staff. UmbraRo is not a fan application, a betting tool, or a generic score-tracking platform. Instead, it is a football intelligence and tactical assistance product built directly on top of the predictive and prescriptive pipeline developed in this thesis. The application is designed around four main pillars. \emph{Prediction} estimates the probability of a home win. \emph{Explanation} highlights the statistical and tactical factors behind the prediction. \emph{Prescription} proposes optimised tactical targets through a constrained simulation-based framework. Finally, \emph{selection} combines per-90 player KPIs with a globally optimal Hungarian assignment to recommend a fixture-specific starting eleven. This fusion of predictive probability, model explanation, tactical prescription, and player-level selection is the central output of the \emph{Match Intelligence and Tactical Blueprint} screen (Section~\ref{subsec:f2_impl}), which is presented in Chapter~\ref{chap:app}.

To make this concrete, consider a Romanian Superliga technical director preparing for an away fixture against the league leader. With three days until kickoff, the staff faces three coupled questions: how likely is a positive result, given the current form of both teams, which eleven players drawn from the available squad give the best on-paper match-up, and which tactical adjustments, on possession share, shots, shots on target, corners, or scoring output, would most realistically raise the probability of a favourable outcome. Off-the-shelf football analytics tools provide partial answers to the first question and almost none to the second and third. UmbraRo is designed precisely to surface all three answers on the same screen, in time for them to influence the actual match-preparation week.

\section{Problem Statement}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

At its core, this thesis addresses two interlocking problems. The first is a binary classification problem: given a fixture in the Romanian Superliga, estimate the probability that the home team will win. Early experiments showed that the original three-class formulation (home win, draw, away win) produced less stable probability estimates, because draws emerge from heterogeneous tactical scenarios and introduce ambiguity into the learning problem. The target was therefore redefined as \emph{Home Win vs.\ Not Home Win}, which produces a more stable learning objective and more reliable probability outputs, and which arguably reflects the binary outcome framing common in coaching practice (favourable outcome or not).

The second problem is prescriptive rather than predictive. A model that outputs a win probability is useful but incomplete. Coaches additionally need to know which tactical dimensions most strongly influence that probability, and which practical adjustments could realistically raise it. This thesis therefore formulates a \emph{constrained tactical optimisation} problem: given a fixture and a calibrated probabilistic classifier, find a tactical blueprint (target values for possession, shots, shots on target, corners, goals scored, and goals conceded) that maximises the home-win probability while staying inside historically realistic bounds (the 5th--95th percentile of each variable's training distribution in the training set) and respecting the hard logical constraint that shots on target be at least $20\%$ of total shots ($\text{SoT} \geq 0.2 \times \text{Shots}$).

\section{Objectives of the Research}
\label{sec:objectives}

The research is guided by four specific objectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Predictive evaluation}. To benchmark a representative set of supervised classifiers on the binary Home-Win-or-Not task using a strict chronological evaluation protocol, time-aware rolling-origin validation, and probability calibration metrics, with the goal of identifying which model offers the highest discriminative power and which offers the most usable probability outputs.
    \item \textbf{Prescriptive framework}. To design a constrained Monte Carlo tactical optimiser that searches the space of feasible tactical configurations, evaluates them through the trained classifier, and returns tactical blueprints associated with measurable probability uplift. The framework is required to remain inside historically realistic ranges, so that the recommendations are interpretable and operationally useful.
    \item \textbf{Player-level selection}. To design a data-driven Starting XI predictor that combines per-90 KPIs, empirical-Bayes shrinkage, and a Hungarian assignment over fine-grained position groups, evaluated by rolling-origin one-fixture-out Jaccard overlap across all sixteen Romanian Superliga 2024-25 clubs.
    \item \textbf{Operational delivery}. To translate the analytical pipeline into a coach-facing mobile and web application, exposing baseline probability, optimised probability, key drivers, tactical blueprint targets, and the recommended starting eleven through a single Match Intelligence screen.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:scope}

The scope of this work is defined as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Competition}. Only the Romanian Superliga is considered. Other European competitions are excluded so that team identities, scheduling patterns, and venue characteristics remain consistent across the training data.
    \item \textbf{Timeframe}. Matches from the seasons 2020--2021 through 2024--2025 are used. Matches before July 1, 2024 are used for model development, and the 2024--2025 season is reserved as a holdout test period.
    \item \textbf{Data granularity}. Team-level outcome modelling is performed on aggregate, pre-match features (Elo ratings, head-to-head context, rest days, rolling tactical averages). Player-level modelling for Starting XI recommendation additionally uses per-match action-level KPIs supplied by Hudl Wyscout (minutes, passes, duels, shots, goals, and defensive actions, aggregated to per-90 rates), but operates strictly on aggregated rather than event-by-event tracking data. Full event-by-event tracking and physiological wearable data fall outside the scope of this study.
    \item \textbf{Rationale for the competition choice}. The Romanian Superliga is deliberately chosen as the study domain rather than a large European league. The competition has only sixteen first-division clubs, no Romanian-language commercial analytics product comparable to the major-league offerings (Statsbomb, Wyscout dashboards, Opta), and a contained five-season window of approximately 1{,}600 matches that fits a thesis-scale dataset while preserving genuine schedule density, promoted-relegated turnover, and tactical heterogeneity. The closed market and underserved coaching staff are the precise conditions in which a model-based tactical-assistance system can demonstrate operational value.
    \item \textbf{Nature of recommendations}. The tactical blueprints produced by the optimiser are model-based suggestions rather than causal guarantees. They identify tactical configurations associated with higher predicted win probability under the trained model, not interventions whose effect is empirically verified on the pitch.
\end{itemize}

\section{Thesis Structure}
\label{sec:structure}

The remainder of the thesis is organised as follows. Chapter~\ref{chap:background} presents the theoretical background, opening with a short football domain primer and then discussing predictive and prescriptive analytics in football together with the machine learning framework that supports the UmbraRo system. Chapter~\ref{chap:approach} describes the proposed approach: an overview that connects the literature to the layered design adopted in this work, followed by the dataset, the training and testing methodology, the experimental results, a player-level modelling layer for Starting XI recommendation, and a discussion. Chapter~\ref{chap:app} presents the practical implementation of UmbraRo, including the system architecture, the main coach-facing screens (the weekly fixture dashboard and the match intelligence sheet), the testing strategy, and a mini user manual. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusions} summarises the contributions of this thesis, synthesises the empirical results, and identifies directions for future work.
