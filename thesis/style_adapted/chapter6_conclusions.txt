\section{Summary of Contributions}
\label{sec:summary_contributions}

This thesis set out to explore and quantify the transition from \emph{predictive} to \emph{prescriptive} analytics in football, using the Romanian Superliga as a study domain. As detailed in Chapters~\ref{chap:approach} and~\ref{chap:app}, the research developed a complete pipeline that begins with raw match data and ends with a deployed coach-facing application. Four contributions are claimed.

First, the prediction task was reformulated from a classical three-class home-win / draw / away-win setting to a binary \emph{Home Win vs.\ Not Home Win} formulation, and benchmarked across seven engineered model families (Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, CatBoost, Support Vector Machines, Naive Bayes, and Multi-Layer Perceptrons, with a single-seed LightGBM run during an earlier iteration producing an accuracy of $61.44\%$, statistically indistinguishable from XGBoost on the same fold, and therefore not carried into the multi-seed comparison), with stochastic models averaged across five random seeds and stress-tested against two trivial baselines (always-home-win and Elo-only logistic regression). The evaluation used a strict chronological holdout, rolling-origin validation, and probability calibration. This experimental design produced an explicit separation between the best \emph{predictive} model (Logistic Regression, $64.89\%$ accuracy) and the best \emph{prescriptive} engine (calibrated CatBoost, with a Brier score of approximately $0.233$ and an Expected Calibration Error of approximately $0.052$). The fact that an Elo-only logistic regression already reaches $65.20\%$ on the same holdout indicates that most predictable signal in pre-match Romanian Superliga data is concentrated in team-strength differentials, and that additional tactical features contribute primarily through calibration and explainability rather than raw accuracy.

Second, a \emph{constrained Monte Carlo tactical optimiser} was introduced as a prescriptive layer on top of the calibrated probabilistic classifier. After vectorisation the optimiser samples $N = 25{,}000$ tactical permutations per fixture in a single batched call, returning the configuration associated with the highest constrained home-win probability under explicit percentile bounds and a hard logical constraint (shots on target $\geq 0.2 \times$ shots) that forbids physically implausible combinations. A retrospective check on the 2024--2025 holdout further showed that home teams whose actual rolling averages were closest to the optimiser's recommendation won at $48.1\%$, compared with $39.4\%$ for the rest of the fixtures, an $8.7$ percentage-point empirical lift. This translation from forecasting to recommendation operationalises the calibrated probability output into a coach-facing tactical plan, with the constrained search ensuring that the returned blueprint stays inside historically realistic percentile bounds and basic football-logic constraints derived from the training distribution.

Third, a player-level Starting XI Recommendation layer complements the team-level pipeline. Trained on a $278$-match Wyscout dataset spanning the Romanian Superliga 2024--2025 season ($16$ clubs, $556$ fixtures, $6{,}056$ actual-starter records and approximately $15{,}396$ (player, fixture) training rows), it combines per-$90$ KPI normalisation, empirical-Bayes shrinkage~\cite{brown2008empirical}, a $\tanh$-bounded composite score, a Hungarian-assignment selection rule~\cite{kuhn1955hungarian}, and a pooled supervised Logistic Regression. Under a strict rolling-origin one-fixture-out cross-validation it reaches a league-wide Jaccard@$11$ of $\mathbf{0.6227}$ across $428$ held-out (team, fixture) cells (Table~\ref{tab:xi_validation_perteam}). An availability-feature ablation quantified that $+10.32$ percentage points of this headline are attributable to autoregressive recency signals rather than to intrinsic per-$90$ KPI quality (Table~\ref{tab:availability_ablation}). Earlier supervised starting-XI work in the public literature has focused on the major European leagues, and this layer extends that methodology to the Romanian Superliga, evaluated across all sixteen 2024-2025 top-flight clubs under a strict rolling-origin one-fixture-out protocol.

Fourth, these academic contributions were delivered to end users through UmbraRo, a Flutter web/mobile application backed by a Python FastAPI service deployed on a managed cloud runtime, with separate managed services for authentication, relational persistence, and static-asset hosting. The Match Intelligence and Tactical Blueprint screen described in Section~\ref{subsec:f2_impl} fuses the predictive, explanatory, and prescriptive layers into a single coach-facing interface, demonstrating that the framework is not only methodologically defensible but operationally usable.

\section{Synthesis of Results}
\label{sec:synthesis_results}

The experimental evaluation conducted in Chapter~\ref{chap:approach} yielded several critical insights into the nature of football outcome modelling.

First, the binary reformulation of the target produced more stable probability estimates than the original multiclass setting, confirming the well-known difficulty of modelling football draws. Second, feature engineering had a major effect on model quality: Elo-based contextual variables, head-to-head context, rest days, and rolling tactical averages provided a representation that consistently outperformed raw historical results across every model family. Third, the experiments revealed a meaningful separation between predictive performance and prescriptive usefulness. Logistic Regression achieved the highest raw test accuracy ($64.89\%$) and a competitive Brier score after sigmoid calibration ($0.2256$), but reacted too aggressively to tactical perturbations inside the optimisation loop. CatBoost, by contrast, produced smoother responses. With the vectorised optimiser at $N = 25{,}000$, the UTA Arad vs.\ FCSB fixture moved from a $22.10\%$ baseline to a $30.76 \pm 0.11\%$ optimised probability (an uplift of approximately $8.7$ percentage points across five seeds), and CFR Cluj vs.\ FCSB moved from $37.82\%$ to $44.14 \pm 0.08\%$ (approximately $6.3$ percentage points). Calibrated CatBoost was retained as the production prescriptive engine despite a marginally lower raw accuracy of approximately $62.95\%$.

Across three rolling-origin folds the pipeline reported a mean accuracy of $0.6254$, a mean log-loss of $0.6650$, and a mean Brier score of $0.2354$. The width of the bootstrap confidence interval for accuracy ($[0.5862, 0.6897]$) reflects the modest size of the holdout season and is consistent with the predictive ceiling reported in the European football prediction literature. The overarching takeaway is that, in football, the best predictive model is not necessarily the best prescriptive model: predictive accuracy and tactical realism are distinct optimisation criteria, and a coach-facing system should be designed around the latter.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

While this thesis establishes a working pipeline from predictive estimation through to deployed prescriptive recommendation, several directions remain open for future research. The single most significant opportunity is the integration of GPS and biometric tracking data, which professional Romanian Superliga clubs already collect through FIFA and UEFA-approved wearables. A representative match report from the FC Universitatea Cluj cup final, for instance, records for every player the total distance, distance per minute, maximum sprint speed, four acceleration-zone counts (decelerations in $[-10, -4]$ and $[-4, -3]$~m\,s$^{-2}$, accelerations in $[3, 4]$ and $[4, 10]$~m\,s$^{-2}$), the distance covered in each of the five FIFA-approved speed zones (0--7, 7--15, 15--20, 20--25, $>$25~km/h), and the metabolic power load in W/kg. None of the predictive or prescriptive layers in this thesis consumes any of this data. The items below describe what they could deliver if they did, together with the broader methodological directions that remain open.

\paragraph{Physical-profile-based player scouting.}
The per-90 KPI composite of Section~\ref{subsec:xi_features} could be augmented with physical features (high-speed running distance, sprint count, acceleration and deceleration counts, metabolic power) so that the supervised classifier and the user-facing similarity queries reason about each player's physical capacity, not only about their action-level efficiency. A concrete query would be \emph{``find a Liga 2 wing-back whose $\text{Z4}$ distance exceeds $800$~m and $\text{Z5}$ distance exceeds $150$~m per match''}, returning candidates whose physical profile matches a club's tactical requirement. This is the natural extension of the position-relative paradigm of Pappalardo et al.~\cite{pappalardo2019playerank} to the physical-performance modality.

\paragraph{Acute-to-chronic workload monitoring for injury prevention.}
Aggregating each player's daily training and matchday loads over rolling 7-day (\emph{acute}) and 28-day (\emph{chronic}) windows yields the acute-to-chronic workload ratio (ACWR) introduced by Gabbett~\cite{gabbett2016training}, which the wider sports-science literature also relates to recovery and availability planning~\cite{suresh2025sports}. Players whose ACWR exceeds approximately $1.5$ have been shown to face a substantially elevated soft-tissue injury risk. A red-yellow-green flag derived from this ratio could be surfaced inside the UmbraRo Starting XI interface, alerting a coach when a candidate they intend to start carries an elevated risk score. Combined with the availability-feature observation in Section~\ref{subsec:xi_availability_ablation}, this would close the loop between coach-decision-driven availability signals and physiologically grounded risk.

\paragraph{Squad rotation under congested calendars.}
The Hungarian assignment of Section~\ref{subsec:xi_selection} extends naturally to multi-fixture windows: rather than maximising composite scores for a single fixture, the optimiser can distribute high-speed running, sprint count and acceleration/deceleration workload across the squad over the next three matches in seven days, subject to a per-player cumulative-load budget. This addresses the congested-calendar problem that European clubs face when they qualify for continental competition during the domestic season.

\paragraph{Tactical-style profiling from physical signatures.}
The $k$-means opponent-style cluster of Section~\ref{subsec:xi_features} currently consumes possession, shots, corners and goals only. Adding aggregate acceleration and deceleration counts (a proxy for pressing intensity) and zone-distance ratios (a proxy for transition versus control style) would yield a richer opponent profile that conditions the supervised XI classifier on physical, not only tactical, opponent characteristics. The cluster identifier already feeds the one-hot encoding consumed by the classifier, so the only methodological work required is the feature-engineering layer.

\paragraph{Extension to additional competitions.}
A cross-competition evaluation against Liga 2, other European national leagues, or the European cup competitions would test whether the constrained Monte Carlo formulation and the supervised Starting XI predictor transfer to environments with different scheduling patterns, tactical norms and squad sizes. The pooled league-wide architecture validated in Section~\ref{subsec:xi_supervised} already demonstrates that the pipeline is not tied to a single club. Whether it is tied to a single competition remains to be established.

\paragraph{Action-level player evaluation via VAEP.}
The player-level layer could be enriched with action-level valuation, for example the VAEP framework of Decroos et al.~\cite{decroos2019vaep}, so that the Starting XI predictor reasons about what each player has contributed to their team's outcomes on the pitch, rather than only about per-$90$ KPI aggregates. Coupled with a per-fixture rolling-origin retraining of the supervised classifier, which would remove the small training-set leakage from the entire-season per-$90$ aggregates acknowledged in Section~\ref{subsec:xi_limitations}, this would tighten the methodological claim of the XI layer.
